## How to Explain the Job to the Contractor

Use this exact phrasing so the technician can quickly assess your materials, local code requirements, and electrical panel capacity:

1. *"I want a general safety inspection of my main service panel to see if I have available physical slots and sufficient amperage capacity for additions."*
2. *"I need to add two new dedicated circuits run to the backyard area."*
3. *"I want a few outdoor outlets installed. Please ensure they use code-compliant GFCI protection with wet-rated, weatherproof 'in-use' flip covers."*
4. *"I need a 12v landscape lighting transformer mounted on the back exterior wall. I want it positioned near one of the new outlets so it can plug right in, keeping the low-voltage line drops clean and easy to bury."*

## Key Recommendations

- **Coordinate Low-Voltage Lines:** Have the electrician mount and plug in the transformer, but do not pay their hourly rate to dig trenches or wire the small landscape lights. You can easily run the low-voltage 12/2 lines and push-pin fixtures yourself later.
- **Inquire About Permitting:** Outdoor additions and new circuit runs typically require local township safety permits. Ask the contractor if they handle the submission and if the permit fees are wrapped into their initial quote.
- **Consolidate Your Outlets:** Electricians frequently drill straight through the back of an existing indoor outlet box to mount a brand-new outdoor outlet directly on the opposite side of the wall. If your layout allows this, it cuts labor costs significantly.
