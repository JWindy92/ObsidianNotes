# Drawing Curriculum — Progressive Milestones

**Overall objective:** Develop the ability to draw convincingly from observation and imagination, construct original subjects, understand form and anatomy, and produce finished black-and-white illustrations.

**Approach:** Work through the milestones progressively, but continue personal drawings throughout. The milestones represent proficiency targets, not strict restrictions on what you can study.

For each concept, use this cycle:
1. **Study:** Read or watch the relevant material and identify the underlying principle.
2. **Replicate:** Complete the instructor's demonstration or exercise.
3. **Reconstruct:** Repeat it from memory without looking.
4. **Vary:** Change the orientation, proportions, subject, or conditions.
5. **Apply:** Use the principle in an original drawing.
6. **Evaluate:** Compare the result against the milestone benchmarks.

For every copied exercise, produce at least one original drawing applying the same principle.

# Milestone 1 — Line Control, Shape Design, and Draftsmanship

**Difficulty:** Beginner → Intermediate

### Learning objectives
- Develop confident, intentional lines and strokes.
- Control straight lines, curves, ellipses, and line weight.
- Understand contour, construction, and gesture lines.
- Simplify complex subjects into basic shapes.
- Recognize and draw positive and negative shapes.
- Develop clear, recognizable silhouettes.
- Reduce unnecessary searching, scratching, and corrective marks.

### Recommended sources
- **Book:** *Keys to Drawing* — Bert Dodson
- **Course:** [Drawabox — Lesson 1](https://drawabox.com/lesson/1)
- **Instructor:** [Peter Han — Dynamic Sketching](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Peter+Han+Dynamic+Sketching)
- **Instructor:** [Alphonso Dunn](https://www.youtube.com/@alphonsodunn)
- **Course:** [Proko — Drawing Basics](https://www.proko.com/course/drawing-basics)

### Exercises
- [ ] Fill two pages with straight lines, curves, and directional strokes.
- [ ] Fill two pages with ellipses of varying orientations and proportions.
- [ ] Draw 10 household objects using simple geometric construction.
- [ ] Draw 10 objects using minimal contour lines.
- [ ] Produce 10 recognizable silhouettes of animals or creatures.
- [ ] Create five original creature concepts using only basic shapes and silhouettes.
- [ ] Repeat selected exercises without construction references.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Lines are intentional, controlled, and placed with reasonable accuracy.
- Ellipses are consistent and communicate the orientation of their forms.
- Complex subjects can be reduced to simple, recognizable shapes.
- Silhouettes communicate the identity and pose of a subject.
- Construction lines support the drawing rather than obscure it.
- Can simplify an unfamiliar subject without losing its essential structure.

# Milestone 2 — Perspective and Three-Dimensional Construction

**Difficulty:** Intermediate → Advanced

### Learning objectives
- Understand horizon lines, vanishing points, and perspective systems.
- Construct objects in one-, two-, and three-point perspective.
- Rotate primitive forms in space.
- Draw boxes, cylinders, cones, and complex objects from imagination.
- Understand ellipses, cross-contours, intersections, and overlapping forms.
- Use perspective to construct architecture, machinery, and environments.
- Develop the ability to draw through forms rather than relying on visible outlines.

### Recommended sources
- **Primary textbook:** *How to Draw* — Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling
- **Course:** [Marshall Vandruff — 1994 Perspective Series](https://marshallart.com/SHOP/all-products/all-videos/1994-perspective-drawing-series/)
- **Exercises:** [Marshall Vandruff — Perspective Projects](https://marshallart.com/SHOP/all-products/all-videos/1994-perspective-drawing-series/perspective-projects/)
- **Course:** [Drawabox — Lesson 2](https://drawabox.com/lesson/2)
- **Instructor:** [Peter Han — Dynamic Sketching](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Peter+Han+Dynamic+Sketching)

### Exercises
- [ ] Construct 20 boxes in varying orientations.
- [ ] Construct 20 cylinders and ellipses in varying orientations.
- [ ] Draw 10 household objects using primitive forms.
- [ ] Construct five complex mechanical or architectural objects.
- [ ] Draw three environments using multiple perspective depths.
- [ ] Invent five unfamiliar objects using only geometric construction.
- [ ] Draw selected objects from multiple viewpoints.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Can construct basic forms in space without relying on a reference.
- Can rotate boxes and cylinders mentally and represent their orientation.
- Ellipses and cross-contours accurately communicate three-dimensional form.
- Complex objects can be broken into and reconstructed from primitives.
- Can construct a simple environment from imagination.
- Understands how perspective changes the apparent proportions of objects.

# Milestone 3 — Organic Forms, Gesture, and Structural Construction

**Difficulty:** Intermediate → Advanced

### Learning objectives
- Understand gesture, flow, and directional movement.
- Construct organic volumes rather than relying on flat outlines.
- Understand tapering, bending, twisting, compression, and extension.
- Connect and overlap organic forms convincingly.
- Use cross-contours to describe organic volume.
- Construct branching structures, tentacles, limbs, and irregular forms.
- Apply geometric construction principles to organic subjects.

### Recommended sources
- **Instructor:** [Peter Han — Dynamic Sketching](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Peter+Han+Dynamic+Sketching)
- **Course:** [Drawabox — Lesson 2: Organic Forms](https://drawabox.com/lesson/2/1)
- **Course:** [Proko — Figure Drawing Fundamentals](https://www.proko.com/course/figure-drawing-fundamentals/)
- **Instructor:** [Glenn Vilppu — Figure Drawing](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Glenn+Vilppu+figure+drawing)
- **Reference and instruction:** [John Muir Laws](https://johnmuirlaws.com/)
- **Book:** *The Art of Animal Drawing* — Ken Hultgren

### Exercises
- [ ] Draw 10 sausage forms with varying bends, twists, and orientations.
- [ ] Draw 10 branching or interlocking organic structures.
- [ ] Construct five tentacles with different bends, twists, and tapering.
- [ ] Construct five amphibian or reptilian bodies from simple volumes.
- [ ] Design five original creatures using combinations of organic forms.
- [ ] Draw selected creatures from at least two viewpoints.
- [ ] Practice connecting limbs and appendages to larger body masses.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Organic forms maintain convincing volume through bends and twists.
- Cross-contours communicate the direction and orientation of forms.
- Connected forms appear structurally attached rather than arbitrarily joined.
- Can construct unfamiliar organic forms using simple volumes.
- Can maintain the basic structure of an invented form across multiple views.
- Gesture communicates movement without sacrificing underlying structure.

# Milestone 4 — Observational Accuracy and Visual Measurement

**Difficulty:** Beginner → Intermediate

> Basic observation should be practiced from the beginning. This milestone represents achieving reliable observational accuracy, not postponing observation until this point.

### Learning objectives
- Develop accurate visual measurement and proportion.
- Compare relative angles, distances, and alignments.
- Recognize positive and negative shapes.
- Improve contour accuracy and feature placement.
- Understand symmetry and asymmetry.
- Learn to observe shapes rather than rely on symbolic representations of familiar objects.
- Identify and correct inaccuracies without tracing.

### Recommended sources
- **Primary textbook:** *Keys to Drawing* — Bert Dodson
- **Course:** [Proko — Learning How to Draw](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/learning-how-to-draw)
- **Instructor:** [John Muir Laws](https://johnmuirlaws.com/)
- **Reference library:** [Line of Action](https://line-of-action.com/)
- **Instructor:** [Stephen Bauman](https://www.youtube.com/@StephenBaumanArt)

### Exercises
- [ ] Draw three household geometric objects from observation.
- [ ] Draw three irregular organic objects from observation.
- [ ] Draw three skulls or anatomical references from different angles.
- [ ] Draw three human heads from different orientations.
- [ ] Practice comparing angles and distances between major landmarks.
- [ ] Draw the same subject repeatedly, correcting proportion and alignment.
- [ ] Complete selected studies using minimal construction lines and no shading.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Major proportions and angles are convincing when compared with the reference.
- Can identify and correct misaligned features independently.
- Can reproduce the same subject with reasonably consistent proportions.
- Can draw a head from front, profile, and three-quarter views without tracing.
- Can identify negative shapes and use them to check proportions.
- Can explain the measurement relationships used to construct a drawing.

# Milestone 5 — Human and Animal Anatomy

**Difficulty:** Advanced

### Learning objectives
- Understand skeletal structure and anatomical landmarks.
- Understand major muscle groups, attachments, and surface anatomy.
- Construct the torso, shoulder girdle, pelvis, limbs, hands, and feet.
- Understand head construction and facial anatomy.
- Apply anatomy to gesture, balance, movement, and foreshortening.
- Understand differences between mammalian, avian, reptilian, and amphibian anatomy.
- Develop believable anatomy for original and hybrid creatures.

### Recommended sources
- **Course:** [Proko — Figure Drawing Fundamentals](https://www.proko.com/course/figure-drawing-fundamentals/)
- **Book:** *Constructive Anatomy* — George Bridgman
- **Book:** *Drawing the Head and Hands* — Andrew Loomis
- **Instructor:** [Glenn Vilppu](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Glenn+Vilppu+anatomy+drawing)
- **Instructor:** [David Finch](https://davidfinchart.com/library/)
- **Book:** *The Art of Animal Drawing* — Ken Hultgren
- **Book:** *The Laws Guide to Drawing Birds* — John Muir Laws

### Exercises
- [ ] Draw five human heads from different angles.
- [ ] Draw five torso studies emphasizing major anatomical masses.
- [ ] Draw five full-body figures in different poses.
- [ ] Complete five hand studies.
- [ ] Study five animal skeleton-to-surface relationships.
- [ ] Construct five original hybrid creatures using recognizable anatomical systems.
- [ ] Draw selected creatures in multiple poses and viewpoints.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Can construct the human figure from simplified anatomical masses.
- Understands major skeletal landmarks and muscle groups.
- Can draw figures in different poses without relying entirely on outline copying.
- Can explain how major anatomical structures affect surface appearance.
- Can create a believable hybrid creature with internally consistent anatomy.
- Can maintain anatomical structure across different poses and viewpoints.

# Milestone 6 — Light, Shadow, and Tonal Structure

**Difficulty:** Intermediate → Advanced

### Learning objectives
- Understand light sources and their relationship to form.
- Distinguish form shadows from cast shadows.
- Understand the terminator, core shadow, reflected light, and halftones.
- Understand occlusion and contact shadows.
- Develop value scales and organize light and shadow groups.
- Control edges and value transitions.
- Understand multiple light sources.
- Apply lighting principles to stylized and imaginative subjects.

### Recommended sources
- **Book:** *Color and Light* — James Gurney
- **Book:** *Light, Shade and Shadow* — E. L. Koller
- **Course:** [Proko — Light, Shadow & Value](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/light-shadow-and-value)
- **Course:** [Proko — Core Shadows](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/core-shadows)
- **Course:** [New Masters Academy — Fundamentals of Light & Shadow](https://www.nma.art/videolessons/the-fundamentals-of-light-shadow/)
- **Course:** [Steven Zapata — Secrets of Shading](https://www.proko.com/course/secrets-of-shading)

### Exercises
- [ ] Shade a sphere, cube, cylinder, cone, and pyramid.
- [ ] Shade five elongated organic forms, such as sausages or tentacles.
- [ ] Shade five interlocking organic forms.
- [ ] Complete five skull studies under different lighting conditions.
- [ ] Complete five anatomical studies using simplified value groups.
- [ ] Shade five original creature designs.
- [ ] Repeat selected studies using only three distinct values.
- [ ] Draw the same object under at least two different light conditions.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Can identify and explain the major components of light and shadow.
- Shadows describe the underlying volume rather than simply darkening outlines.
- Cast shadows are consistent with the light source.
- Can organize a drawing into convincing light and shadow groups.
- Can shade an invented object using a deliberate lighting setup.
- Rendering does not obscure or compromise the underlying construction.

# Milestone 7 — Rendering, Texture, and Mark-Making

**Difficulty:** Intermediate → Advanced

### Learning objectives
- Develop control over hatching and crosshatching.
- Understand cross-contour and form-following linework.
- Control line density, spacing, thickness, and taper.
- Explore stippling, engraving, and scratchboard techniques.
- Understand solid-black shadow shapes and negative space.
- Develop material-specific textures.
- Combine rendering techniques to produce intentional visual effects.
- Understand the relationship between mark-making and value.

### Recommended sources
- **Book:** *Pen and Ink Drawing* — Alphonso Dunn
- **Book:** *Rendering in Pen and Ink* — Arthur L. Guptill
- **Book:** *The Art of Comic Book Inking* — Gary Martin
- **Tutorial:** [Art Rocket — Inking Superhero Comics](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/159080)
- **Lesson:** [Mitchell Albala — Exploring Composition Through Shape and Notan](https://mitchalbala.com/video-exploring-composition-though-shape-notan/)
- **Course:** [Proko — Crosshatching](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/crosshatching)
- **Instructor:** [Alphonso Dunn](https://www.youtube.com/@alphonsodunn)
- **Course:** [SVSLearn — Crosshatching in Ink](https://courses.svslearn.com/courses/crosshatching-in-ink)

### Artists to study
- Albrecht Dürer — controlled hatching and anatomical detail.
- Gustave Doré — dramatic tonal structures and dense crosshatching.
- Bernie Wrightson — organic anatomy and horror illustration.
- Sergio Toppi — expressive linework and texture.
- Franklin Booth — intricate line density.
- Virgil Finlay — fine-line rendering and organic textures.
- Mike Mignola — solid-black shapes and negative space.
- Hugo Pratt — economical rendering and deliberate black shapes.

### Exercises
- [ ] Create a series of hatching and crosshatching value scales.
- [ ] Render a skull using conventional pencil shading.
- [ ] Render the same skull using crosshatching.
- [ ] Render the same skull using stippling.
- [ ] Render the same skull using solid-black spot shading.
- [ ] Render the same skull using an engraving-inspired technique.
- [ ] Create texture studies for skin, scales, bone, stone, metal, and organic surfaces.
- [ ] Complete one finished creature illustration combining at least two rendering techniques.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Can control value through mark-making rather than relying on smudging.
- Hatching follows the underlying form and communicates volume.
- Can distinguish materials through appropriate texture and mark selection.
- Solid-black shapes communicate form, lighting, and composition.
- Can maintain consistent tonal effects across a finished illustration.
- Rendering enhances the construction and readability of the drawing.

# Milestone 8 — Composition, Visual Hierarchy, and Graphic Design

**Difficulty:** Advanced

### Learning objectives
- Understand positive and negative space.
- Develop strong figure-ground relationships.
- Use silhouettes and shape language intentionally.
- Establish focal points and visual hierarchy.
- Understand balance, asymmetry, rhythm, and repetition.
- Explore cropping, framing, and visual storytelling.
- Organize value patterns before introducing detail.
- Use composition to communicate mood, atmosphere, and narrative.

### Recommended sources
- **Book:** *Keys to Drawing* — Bert Dodson
- **Book:** *Notan: The Dark-Light Principle of Design* — Dorr Bothwell & Marlys Mayfield
- **Lesson:** [Mitchell Albala — Exploring Composition Through Shape and Notan](https://mitchalbala.com/video-exploring-composition-though-shape-notan/)
- **Book:** *Imaginative Realism* — James Gurney
- **Instructor:** [FZD School — Design Cinema](https://www.youtube.com/@FZDSCHOOL)

### Artists to study
- Gustave Doré — dramatic composition and visual storytelling.
- Mike Mignola — silhouette and graphic hierarchy.
- Sergio Toppi — unconventional framing and visual rhythm.
- Bernie Wrightson — atmosphere and detailed compositions.
- Aubrey Beardsley — shape design and negative space.

### Exercises
- [ ] Create six thumbnail compositions for an original portrait.
- [ ] Create six thumbnail compositions for a creature and environment.
- [ ] Create six thumbnail compositions for a multi-subject narrative scene.
- [ ] Reduce each composition to simple black-and-white shapes.
- [ ] Experiment with different focal points, cropping, and negative space.
- [ ] Complete one finished illustration based on the strongest thumbnail.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Composition remains readable at thumbnail size.
- Focal points and visual hierarchy are deliberate.
- Negative space contributes to the design.
- Black-and-white value patterns are balanced and intentional.
- Can produce multiple distinct compositions for the same subject.
- Can communicate mood and narrative without relying on excessive detail.

# Milestone 9 — Imaginative Construction and Visual Memory

**Difficulty:** Advanced

### Learning objectives
- Develop the ability to draw convincingly from imagination.
- Improve visual memory and spatial reasoning.
- Combine multiple references into original designs.
- Maintain structural consistency across viewpoints.
- Mentally rotate and reconstruct complex forms.
- Invent believable anatomy and creature structures.
- Apply perspective, anatomy, lighting, and composition without direct copying.
- Develop independent problem-solving and a personal visual language.

### Exercises
- [ ] Create three original creature concepts without copying an existing design.
- [ ] Develop a structural framework for the strongest concept.
- [ ] Construct its major anatomical masses and appendages.
- [ ] Draw the creature from front, side, and three-quarter viewpoints.
- [ ] Draw the creature in three distinct poses.
- [ ] Establish consistent lighting across the drawings.
- [ ] Produce a finished black-and-white creature illustration.

### Proficiency benchmarks
- Original subjects are structurally convincing.
- Anatomy is internally consistent and believable.
- Can maintain volume and proportions across multiple viewpoints.
- Poses communicate weight, movement, and physical structure.
- Lighting and rendering are convincing without relying on direct copying.
- Composition and visual hierarchy are deliberate.
- Can explain the construction of an original subject and identify and correct structural errors independently.

# Final Proficiency Assessment

Complete six portfolio drawings demonstrating the skills developed throughout the curriculum.

1. **Observational still life:** Accurate proportions, construction, and tonal control.
2. **Mechanical object:** Perspective, geometric construction, and spatial reasoning.
3. **Human figure:** Gesture, anatomy, proportion, and movement.
4. **Animal or amphibian:** Organic construction, anatomy, and surface structure.
5. **Original complex creature:** Imaginative construction, anatomy, and multiple viewpoints.
6. **Finished narrative illustration:** Composition, visual hierarchy, lighting, and rendering.

### Self-evaluation rubric

| Score | Standard |
|---|---|
| 1 | Cannot reliably demonstrate the skill. |
| 2 | Understands the concept but applies it inconsistently. |
| 3 | Can demonstrate the skill with references or guidance. |
| 4 | Can apply the skill independently and reliably. |
| 5 | Can adapt and combine the skill in unfamiliar situations. |

### Evaluation categories
- [ ] Observational accuracy
- [ ] Line control and draftsmanship
- [ ] Perspective and construction
- [ ] Organic forms and gesture
- [ ] Human and animal anatomy
- [ ] Light and shadow
- [ ] Rendering and mark-making
- [ ] Composition and visual hierarchy
- [ ] Imaginative construction

**Target:** Score at least 4 in every category before considering the curriculum complete.

This is a personal proficiency framework, not a standardized qualification. The objective is independent, repeatable application—not simply completing every exercise.

# Ongoing Study Principles

- Maintain a dated fundamentals sketchbook.
- Continue original creature, character, and environment drawings throughout every milestone.
- Study artists by analyzing their decisions, not just copying their finished work.
- Use references to understand structure, not as a substitute for understanding it.
- Revisit earlier milestones whenever a weakness becomes apparent.
- Prioritize construction and form capture before extensive rendering.
- Avoid treating the milestones as isolated subjects. Perspective, anatomy, observation, and composition should increasingly work together.
- Periodically repeat earlier exercises to measure improvement.

**The ultimate goal is not to become proficient at completing drawing exercises. It is to develop the underlying skills needed to independently conceive, construct, and finish original artwork.**
